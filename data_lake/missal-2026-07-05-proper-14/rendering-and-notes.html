This document is a first-pass reconstruction of the printed missal (the entire 12-page order of service is one missal, transcribed) plus structural notes for liturgical world modelling. It renders the shape of the service and its texts-by-reference; it is not the full printed word-for-word text. Hymn/setting attributions are from the missal.

1 · The shape of the service

This is a Western eucharistic liturgy in the fourfold ecumenical ordo that the Canadian Book of Alternative Services shares with the Roman, Lutheran, and Episcopal rites. Four movements, each with a hinge action:

MovementHingeWhat happens
GatheringAssembling & praise Procession, greeting, land acknowledgement, Gloria, Collect — the assembly is constituted and turns toward God.
WordReading & response Three lessons + psalm, Gospel, Sermon, Creed, Intercessions, Confession, Peace — Scripture proclaimed and answered.
Table (Eucharist)Take, bless, break, give Offertory, Great Thanksgiving, Lord's Prayer, Fraction, Communion — the four dominical actions over bread and wine.
SendingBlessing & commission Commissioning of lay ministers, Blessing, Closing Hymn, Dismissal — the assembly is sent out.
STRUCTURE The engine of the rite is the split between Ordinary (fixed sung texts that recur every Sunday — Gloria, Sanctus, Agnus Dei) and Proper (what changes with the day — Collect, readings, psalm, sermon, propers of music). Modelling a liturgy means modelling this two-track structure: a stable frame with slots the day's Proper is plugged into.

I. The Gathering of the Community

Processional Hymn. Joyful, Joyful, We Adore Thee (Common Praise 425).
Greeting & Land Acknowledgement. Presider/All dialogue. Worship on the traditional, ancestral, and unceded territory of the xʲməθkʲəy̌əm (Musqueam), Sḷẃx̲wú7mesh (Squamish), and Səľílwətaʔɯ (Tsleil-Waututh) Nations.

The Collect of the Day. Silence is kept. “Almighty God, your Son Jesus Christ has taught us that what we do for the least of your children we do also for him…” — the day's Proper prayer, gathering the theme.

II. The Proclamation of the Word

First Reading. Genesis 24:34–38, 42–49, 58–67 — Word of God, word of life. / Thanks be to God.
Psalm 45:11–18. Sung with refrain “Consider and listen closely for the voice of God” (Gradual Psalms for the RCL, ed. Bruce E. Ford).
Second Reading. Romans 7:15–25a — Hear what the Spirit is saying to the churches. / Thanks be to God.
Sequence Hymn. Lord, Your Word Shall Guide Us (CP 561).
The Holy Gospel. Matthew 11:16–19, 25–30 — “Come to me, all you that are weary…”
The Sermon. The Rev. Dr. Richard Topping.
The Apostles' Creed. Baptismal symbol, said by all.
The Prayers of the People. Intercessions (Intercessor: Jane Hope).
The Confession and Absolution. Silence may be kept. “…We confess that we have sinned against you… in thought, and word, and deed…”
The Peace. All may exchange a sign of peace. Hinge from Word to Table.

III. The Celebration of the Eucharist

The Offertory. Gifts of bread and wine + stewardship presented at the altar.
Offertory Hymn. Alleluia! Sing to Jesus (CP 374; Hyfrydol).
The Prayer over the Gifts.
The Great Thanksgiving. Eucharistic Prayer 3 (Enriching Our Worship). Opens with the Sursum corda dialogue (“Lift up your hearts / We lift them to the Lord”).
Sanctus. (Hassler, Missa super Dixit Maria) — sung ordinary.
Memorial Acclamation & Great Amen. (music: Neil Cockburn).
The Lord's Prayer. (setting: McNeil Robinson).
The Breaking of the Bread — Fraction Anthem. Lamb of God (CP 690; New Plainsong, David Hurd).
The Communion. Reception rubric: approach the altar, hands extended for the Bread; guide the chalice to your lips, or intinct is not permitted for public health; a blessing is available; healing prayer offered in the west alcove.
During Communion. If Ye Love Me (Thomas Tallis, 1505?–1585); I Come with Joy (CP 60; Land of Rest).
The Prayer after Communion & Doxology.

IV. The Sending Forth of the Community

Commissioning. Lay ministers sent out to take communion to the absent.
The Blessing.
Closing Hymn. The Church of Christ in Every Age (CP 584).
The Dismissal. — Thanks be to God.
Postlude. Finale from Suite Gothique / Suite 3 for Organ — Léon Boëllmann (1862–1897).

3 · Modelling notes — what the missal compresses

A missal is a lossy, high-compression encoding of a ritual: it records only enough to cue people who already hold the conventions. Modelling it means decompressing — recovering the enacted service the document assumes. These notes flag where compression happens and what the decompression key is.

DECOMPRESSION TARGET What the missal omits (and the ritual has). None of this is printed, yet all of it happens: posture (when the assembly stands / sits / kneels), movement (processions — entrance, Gospel, offertory, communion; who goes where), ceremonial action (censing, the fraction gesture, elevations, the exchange of peace), timing & pacing (length of silences, when music covers action), spatial staging (the "stations" the back page names — Gospel Station, Presider Station — imply locations in the building), and vesture / objects. Decompressing this missal = filling these slots from Anglican/BAS convention. This is the core modelling work.

CONTEXT KEY Pointers that only resolve with external tables. “Proper 14” is a single token that expands to a whole set of propers (collect + three lessons + psalm) — but only via the Canadian BAS calendar the missal assumes and does not include. (The same Sunday is numbered “Proper 9” in the US/Episcopal RCL — same readings, different label.) Likewise “CP 425” is a pointer into Common Praise, and the sung settings name composers, not notes. A large part of the missal is references whose targets live in external books — the model needs those lookup tables.

COMPRESSION LEVELS The same content at two resolutions. The lections appear twice: the missal prints only citations (Genesis 24:34-38…), while the separate Readings PDF carries the full text. That pairing is a worked example of the compression axis this project models — citation (pointer) vs. full text (expanded) — the same ritual content encoded at two levels.

LECTIONARY Year A, Track 1. July 2026 falls in RCL Year A (Matthew), which the Gospel confirms. The Genesis 24 first reading (Isaac and Rebekah) is the semicontinuous Track 1 Old Testament stream, paired here with Psalm 45 (a royal wedding psalm) — note the deliberate nuptial echo between lesson and psalm. Romans 7 is the Epistle's own semicontinuous track, independent of the Gospel theme.

MUSIC Layered sources. The sung Ordinary is split across idioms: Gloria and Agnus Dei from David Hurd's New Plainsong (modern chant), but the Sanctus from Hassler's Renaissance Missa super Dixit Maria — a mixed setting, not a single unified mass. Hymnody is congregational (Common Praise); the choir carries the Latin-tradition polyphony (Tallis at communion). For modelling: “the music” is not one slot but several, each with its own provenance and performer.

STRUCTURE Dialogic backbone. The whole rite runs on fixed call-and-response pairs (Presider → All): the greeting, the Peace, the Sursum corda, the acclamations, the dismissal. These versicle/response units are the connective tissue between the larger blocks — a good primitive to model as a distinct type from the monologue prayers and the sung ordinary.
