# Two missals, one rite — Proper 13 vs Proper 14

Comparing the two decompressed models one week apart at the same parish
(`ccc-2026-06-28-proper-13` and `ccc-2026-07-05-proper-14`). The point: separate the
**ordinary** (the fixed frame) from the **proper** (what changes), and measure how the
missal's own **compression varies** even within one parish.

## The fixed frame (ordinary / structure) — identical both weeks

Same fourfold shape and the same unit sequence: Gathering (procession, hymn, gathering
dialogue, Gloria, Collect) → Word (First Reading, Psalm, Second Reading, Sequence Hymn,
Gospel, Sermon, Creed, Prayers, Confession, Peace) → Eucharist (Offertory + hymn, Prayer
over the Gifts, Great Thanksgiving, Sanctus, institution, Lord's Prayer, Fraction + anthem,
Communion, Prayer after Communion, Doxology) → Sending (Commissioning, Blessing, Closing
Hymn, Dismissal, Postlude). Fixed elements:

- **Gloria** — New Plainsong (David Hurd), CP 686 — both weeks.
- **Lord's Prayer** — McNeil Robinson setting — both weeks.
- **Reception rubric** at Communion — identical wording (common cup, no intinction, blessing
  available, healing prayer in the west alcove).
- **Sursum corda** and the dialogic backbone (greeting, Peace, acclamations, dismissal).
- **Deacon:** The Rev. Kevin de la Mare — both weeks.

## The proper (what changes week to week)

| Element | Proper 13 (Jun 28) | Proper 14 (Jul 5) |

| Presider | **Pappas** (Dean) | **St-Denis** |
| Preacher | **St-Denis** | **Topping** (guest, VST President) |
| Organist / ensemble | **Cockburn** (DoM) + **Cathedral Choir** | **Robertson** (guest) + **"Cathedral Quartet"** (summer) |

| First Reading | Genesis 22:1-14 (Aqedah) | Genesis 24:34-67 (Isaac & Rebekah) |

| Processional | The Love of Jesus Calls Us (CP 434) | Joyful, Joyful (CP 425) |
| Sequence | Thee Will I Love (CP 441) | Lord, Your Word Shall Guide Us (CP 561) |
| Offertory hymn | O Jesus, I Have Promised (CP 438) | Alleluia! Sing to Jesus (CP 374) |

So the "ordinary" is fixed in **shape** but its **music is itself proper** — the sung
Sanctus/Agnus and even the eucharistic prayer swap out week to week. The gathering rite is
more variable than a textbook ordo suggests.

## Compression varies between the two missals

The same parish, printing the same rite, compresses *different things* each week. What one
missal omits, the other prints — which is exactly what lets one missal decompress another:

| Element | Proper 13 | Proper 14 | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sacring bell** | **PRINTED** — "Ringing of the bell. (x3)" | omitted | Parish rings the bell → P14's omission is compression; the bell is now a *justified inference* in the P14 model. |
| Processional hymn text | full text printed | pointer (title + CP no.) | Two compression levels of the same hymn slot. |
| Apostles' Creed | full text printed | pointer | Same. |
| Gospel acclamation | full dialogue printed | compressed | Same dialogic unit, different resolution. |
| Announcements line | printed | omitted | Minor unit present/absent in the *document*, not the service. |
| Silence after sermon | printed ("a period of silent reflection…") | omitted | The silence happens both weeks; only one missal records it. |

## Modelling takeaways

1. **The ordinary is a fixed frame with variable fills.** Model it as stable slots; the
   music-proper (settings, even the eucharistic prayer) plugs into those slots and varies.
2. **A missal's compression is not fixed** — it varies week to week even in one parish.
   Comparing sibling missals is the cheapest source of decompression: what's cued in one is
   printed in another.
3. **Cross-missal inference is legitimate and citable.** The P14 sacring-bell event is
   marked `inferred` with its convention pointing at the P13 missal — a worked example of
   using the corpus to fill a single document's gaps.

Counts (from the models): **Proper 13 — 40 events** (printed 24 / pointer 12 / inferred 4);
**Proper 14 — 42 events** (printed 21 / pointer 14 / inferred 7). Proper 14 leans more on
pointers and needs more inference (summer brevity — including the omitted bell); Proper 13
prints more in full (creed, hymn texts, Gospel dialogue, the bell).
